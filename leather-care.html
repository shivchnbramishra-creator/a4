Caring for exotic leather bags

Everyday habits, storage, cleaning, conditioning and professional care that keep alligator, crocodile and embossed leather looking beautiful for years.

Every day

Small habits, big difference

  • Handle with clean, dry hands; lotions and oils can stain.
  • Set the bag down on clean, dry surfaces, never on wet ground.
  • Avoid overfilling, which stretches the skin and distorts the shape.
  • Keep pens, keys and cosmetics in a pouch inside.
  • Wipe gently with a soft, dry, lint-free cloth after use, following the direction of the scales.

Storage

Storing your bag between wears

  1. Empty it completely and wipe away dust.
  2. Stuff gently with acid-free tissue to hold the shape.
  3. Wrap hardware in tissue to prevent scratches on the skin.
  4. Place in a breathable cotton dust bag, never plastic.
  5. Store upright on a shelf in a cool, dry, dark place with steady humidity.

Avoid hanging bags by their handles for long periods; it can stretch and deform them.

If it gets wet

Blot immediately with a soft, absorbent cloth. Stuff with tissue and let it dry naturally, away from heaters and sun.

If it’s stained

Don’t rub or use household cleaners. Take it to a specialist who works with exotic leather.

If it smells musty

Air it in a well-ventilated room, away from sun. Store with a breathable bag and a cedar block nearby.

Conditioning

Keeping the skin supple

Exotic skins can dry out over time, especially in heated rooms. Use only a conditioner made specifically for exotic leather, and always test on a hidden area first.

Apply a very small amount with a soft cloth, working in the direction of the scales, then buff gently. Do not condition glazed finishes yourself; ask a specialist.

Repairs

When to see a specialist

  • Corners or edges are worn through or the edge paint is cracking.
  • Stitching is loose or a handle feels weak.
  • Hardware is tarnished, stiff or loose.
  • Scales are lifting, scratched or discoloured.

Specialist repairers can often restore colour, re-stitch, replace hardware and re-glaze finishes, extending a bag’s life by many years.
